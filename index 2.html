01 / Project brief

One goal. Several engineering paths.

The challenge is to convert the small movement or force created by an object's weight into a stable electrical signal, then calculate and display the mass. The sensing element and mechanical structure must work together.

MEASUREMENT

Target range

Designed around objects between 100 grams and 1,000 grams. The final prototype must be calibrated across this range.

CONSTRAINT

No load cells

The design exploration excludes commercial load cells. Confirm whether the contest also excludes strain gauges or force-sensing assemblies that function as load cells.

SUCCESS CRITERIA

Repeatable readings

Good repeatability, low drift, stable zero, safe construction, and documented calibration matter as much as the display resolution.

02 / Sensor library

Explore the sensing technologies.

These are candidate technologies, not a claim that every one is suitable for accurate static weighing. Filter the library and compare their roles.

PROMISING

Capacitive displacement sensor

Measures a change in capacitance as a moving plate approaches a fixed plate. A spring mechanism can translate weight into a small displacement.

  • Can detect very small movements
  • Needs stable geometry and shielding
  • Humidity, dust, and parasitic capacitance can affect readings
PROMISING

Optical displacement sensor

Uses reflected light, a light gate, or an optical encoder to measure platform or spring movement.

  • No physical electrical contact at the sensing point
  • Can pair with a spring or lever
  • Alignment and ambient light need control
PROMISING

Hall-effect sensor + magnet

Reads magnetic-field changes as a magnet moves with a spring-supported platform.

  • Compact and inexpensive options exist
  • Requires careful calibration and magnet alignment
  • Nearby magnets and temperature can affect results
PROMISING

Linear potentiometer

Converts linear movement into a changing resistance or voltage in a spring-and-slider mechanism.

  • Simple signal conditioning
  • Easy to demonstrate and understand
  • Wiper friction, wear, and resolution may limit precision
LIMITED

Force-sensitive resistor (FSR)

Resistance changes when pressure is applied. Useful for detecting touch or approximate force, but not usually ideal for precision weighing.

  • Thin and easy to integrate
  • Nonlinear response and hysteresis
  • Drift and unit-to-unit variation can be significant
LIMITED

Piezoelectric sensor

Produces charge when its material is mechanically stressed. Strong for dynamic force or impact detection.

  • Useful for detecting taps or changing loads
  • Charge can decay under a constant load
  • Generally unsuitable as the only sensor for stable static mass readings
LIMITED

Ultrasonic distance sensor

Measures distance using sound pulses. It could observe platform movement, but typical modules may lack the resolution and stability needed for small deflections.

  • Simple non-contact demonstration
  • Beam spread and temperature affect readings
  • Often too coarse for precision weighing
COMPLEX

Inductive displacement sensor

Detects the position of a conductive target through electromagnetic effects. Can measure movement without contact.

  • Robust sensing approach
  • Requires suitable circuitry and calibration
  • Metal target geometry affects response
COMPLEX

Pressure transducer

Measures pressure in a sealed fluid or air chamber. A mechanical platform could transfer force to the chamber.

  • Can measure sustained pressure
  • Leaks, temperature, and seal friction matter
  • More difficult mechanical construction
COMPLEX

MEMS accelerometer

Measures acceleration, not mass directly. In a carefully controlled setup it may detect tilt or vibration, but it is not a straightforward static weighing sensor.

  • Useful for tilt and motion compensation
  • Cannot directly determine static weight on its own
  • Best treated as an auxiliary sensor
COMPLEX

Optical encoder

Counts rotation or position changes in a lever or geared mechanism that moves with the platform.

  • Good for measuring repeatable motion
  • Requires a mechanical displacement stage
  • Backlash and quantization affect accuracy
LIMITED

Flex / bend sensor

Resistance changes as the strip bends. It could sense frame deflection, but response consistency and calibration may be challenging.

  • Simple flexible form factor
  • Nonlinear and affected by fatigue
  • Not a first choice for precision weighing
Competition-rule checkpoint: A strain gauge bonded to a flexure can be the sensing element inside a load-cell-style force transducer. Because the rules ban load cells, do not assume a strain-gauge design is allowed. Ask the organizers for a written ruling before using strain gauges or any force-transducer assembly.
03 / Mechanical frame library

The frame is part of the measurement.

A sensor cannot rescue a frame that binds, twists, rubs, or moves unpredictably. These are structural concepts to evaluate, not finished construction drawings.

GOOD STARTING POINT

Four-post guided platform

A top plate travels vertically on four guide posts, with springs supporting the platform.

  • Helps reduce sideways movement
  • Needs low-friction, well-aligned guides
  • Uneven loading can cause binding
GOOD STARTING POINT

Single central spring platform

A platform is supported by a centered spring and guided to move vertically.

  • Simple force-to-displacement concept
  • Compact mechanical layout
  • Needs anti-tilt guidance and spring characterization
ALTERNATIVE

Lever-arm frame

A platform applies force to one end of a pivoted lever; the other end creates a measurable movement.

  • Can amplify small movement
  • Pivot friction affects repeatability
  • Requires a stiff, stable base
ALTERNATIVE

Parallel-link platform

Linked arms keep the platform approximately level as it moves vertically.

  • Can control platform orientation
  • Joint play affects readings
  • More parts and alignment work
ALTERNATIVE

Flexure-guided platform

Thin compliant members guide a small controlled movement without conventional sliding joints.

  • No sliding-bearing friction
  • Requires careful material and geometry design
  • Fatigue and elastic limits must be considered
ALTERNATIVE

Suspended platform

The weighing plate hangs from a stable support using springs or elastic elements.

  • Clear visual demonstration of displacement
  • Can swing or rotate if poorly guided
  • Needs damping and stable suspension points
ALTERNATIVE

Three-point support frame

Three support points define a stable plane beneath a platform.

  • Avoids rocking from four uneven feet
  • Load distribution still needs consideration
  • Sensor placement must represent total load
ALTERNATIVE

Crossbeam / cantilever frame

A beam deflects slightly under a platform load; a non-contact displacement sensor reads the movement.

  • Simple beam mechanics to model
  • Deflection depends on material and geometry
  • Do not add a prohibited load-cell assembly
ALTERNATIVE

Enclosed cartridge frame

A removable internal module contains the spring, guides, and sensor, protected by an outer housing.

  • Protects moving parts
  • Easy to service and redesign
  • Housing must not touch the moving platform
04 / Concept studio

Different ways to build the scale.

Use these concepts as starting points for prototypes. A fair comparison should use the same reference masses, test procedure, and environmental conditions.

01

Spring + optical displacement

Platform movement compresses a spring. An optical sensor measures the displacement, and calibration maps displacement to mass. Strong candidate for a clear, explainable prototype.

TOP CANDIDATE
02

Spring + Hall-effect sensor

A magnet moves with the guided platform while a Hall sensor measures field change. Compact and visually interesting, but magnet alignment and temperature need testing.

TOP CANDIDATE
03

Spring + capacitive plates

Platform displacement changes the gap between capacitor plates. Potentially sensitive, but electrical shielding and mechanical stability are important.

TOP CANDIDATE
04

Lever + optical encoder

A lever converts platform force into angular movement. An encoder measures the angle or position, then a calibration curve estimates mass.

MECHANICAL
05

Spring + linear potentiometer

A guided slider follows platform movement and changes the potentiometer output. Easy to prototype, although friction and wear may limit repeatability.

LOW COMPLEXITY
06

Fluid chamber + pressure sensor

A platform transfers force to a sealed fluid or air chamber. A pressure transducer reads pressure. This is more mechanically demanding and needs leak and temperature tests.

HIGH COMPLEXITY

05 / Electronics architecture

From sensor signal to readable mass.

The electronics depend on the chosen sensor. This is a modular checklist, not a claim that every component is required in every design.

PROCESSING

Microcontroller

Examples: Arduino-compatible board, ESP32, or another approved microcontroller. Reads sensor data, applies calibration, and updates the display.

SIGNAL CONDITIONING

Amplifier / interface

May be needed for small analog signals. Select the circuit for the sensor's output type, range, noise, and impedance.

CONVERSION

ADC

An analog-to-digital converter samples analog voltage. Resolution alone does not guarantee accuracy; noise, reference stability, and sensor behavior matter.

DISPLAY

LCD / OLED

Shows the calculated mass, units, zero status, and overload or out-of-range messages.

POWER

Regulated supply

Use a stable, appropriately rated supply. Separate noisy loads from sensitive analog sensing where practical.

USER INPUT

Buttons

Useful controls include tare/zero, unit selection, calibration mode, and a menu for test readings.

06 / Recommended direction

Start with a guided spring platform.

A possible first prototype is a guided platform supported by a characterized spring, with a non-contact displacement sensor. This is a hypothesis to test, not a verified best design. It makes the measurement principle visible and avoids relying on a commercial load cell.

FIRST PROTOTYPE

Optical displacement

Best for a clear demonstration of platform movement. Check sensor resolution, repeatability, alignment, and ambient-light rejection.

SECOND PROTOTYPE

Hall-effect displacement

Compare a magnet-and-Hall arrangement against the optical approach using the same mechanical platform and reference masses.

THIRD PROTOTYPE

Capacitive displacement

Explore whether a stable plate arrangement provides a useful signal without excessive sensitivity to wiring, humidity, or nearby objects.

Important: This is a design recommendation, not a verified accuracy claim. No sensor or frame can be called accurate until the assembled prototype has been tested and calibrated. Get the contest organizers to approve the exact sensing principle before buying parts.
07 / Testing plan

Prove the readings, don't just display them.

Build a repeatable test process and report actual results. Use known reference masses and document uncertainty.

Zero check

Record the empty-platform reading before each test series.

Calibration points

Use several known masses distributed across 100–1,000 g, not just one point.

Repeatability

Repeat each measurement and compare the spread of readings.

Load position

Test center and off-center placements to reveal tilt or frame sensitivity.

Hysteresis

Compare readings while increasing and decreasing the applied mass.

Drift

Hold a reference mass and observe whether the reading changes over time.

Environment

Check sensitivity to temperature, vibration, light, and nearby magnetic objects as relevant.

Report honestly

Show error, repeatability, limitations, and the method used to calibrate.
